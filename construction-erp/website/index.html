A model you can actually read

Authoring tools export a colour per object class. Open a real structural model and you get confetti. Majal draws one material and the edges instead — then colours only when you ask a question.

What you get

Six screens that carry most of the work. Each one is real — these are captures from a running system.

Where the money is

Contract value, variations, retention held and work certified but not yet billed — across every project, on one screen.

Read more

Retention is cash sitting with your client; certified-not-invoiced is work you have earned and not yet asked for. Both are usually discovered at month end from a spreadsheet. Here they are a live figure per project, ordered by how far variations have moved the contract.

One approval queue

Every document waiting on you — variation, claim, permit — in a single list with the value at stake.

Read more

Rules are thresholds: below a figure one signature, above it three. The check runs on the server rather than on whether a button was drawn, so it holds whether the request came from the screen, a script or the API. An approver going on leave hands over their own authority and nobody else's.

The bill, priced and tracked

Sections and lines with sell and cost side by side, so margin is visible before the job starts.

Read more

Approved variations append to the bill as new lines rather than editing the original, so the contract value moves and the audit trail survives. Certified quantities write back from each payment certificate, which is what makes cost value reconciliation possible without a second spreadsheet.

The portfolio at a glance

Live projects, contract value and percentage certified, programme slip, open defects, SLA breaches.

Read more

Each figure is a door: the dashboard leads straight into the records behind it, filtered by your company and your role. There are separate views for an owner, construction operations, facilities and commercial, because those four people are not asking the same question.

Assets that answer a phone

Every asset carries a QR and NFC tag. Scan it on site and you are on its record.

Read more

The tag holds a secret token rather than a database id, so a sticker cannot be guessed at or moved onto another asset, and it can be rotated if a batch of labels goes astray. Scans are recorded — who, when, from which tag — which turns a sticker into evidence that somebody actually attended.

Built for a phone in a stairwell

Raising a snag is a photo, a location and a severity — one screen, no scrolling.

Read more

The photo comes first because it is what you walked over to take. Everything decided later — who fixes it, by when, under which trade — is a tab away, where it costs the office nothing and the site nothing. Installs to a home screen as its own app.
