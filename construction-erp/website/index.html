The commercial spine a construction business actually runs on.

Majal is a construction and facilities management ERP built on Odoo 18 Community. Bills of quantities, variations, payment certificates and cost value reconciliation sit on the same database as the RFIs, defects, permits and work orders that generate them — so the number the board sees and the number the site produced are the same number.

What Majal is

Field tools know what happened on site. Finance systems know what was invoiced. Between them sits the part nobody sells you: the bill of quantities the job was priced from, the variations that moved it, the subcontracts that committed the cost, and the monthly reconciliation that says whether the margin the job was tendered at still exists.

Majal is that middle layer, built as Odoo modules so the accounting, purchasing, inventory and HR underneath it are already integrated rather than interfaced. A defect raised on a drawing can become a back-charge on a subcontractor's payment certificate. A variation approved by the board appends its lines to the bill of quantities and appears in the next interim payment certificate. Nothing is re-keyed.

Who it is for

Main contractors
BOQ-priced work, interim payment certificates with retention, subcontract packages, variations, CVR and site records that stand up in a claim.
Developers and consultants
Drawing registers with revision control, RFIs and submittals with ball-in-court, tender packages levelled line by line, and portfolio-level commercial exposure.
Facilities operators
Asset registry with a real location hierarchy, preventive maintenance that generates its own work orders, SLAs measured on a business calendar, spare parts drawn from real stores, and maintenance contracts that show their margin.

Six things worth looking at closely

Commercial control

BOQ → variations → certificates → CVR

A hierarchical bill of quantities with a material / labour / equipment / subcontract / overhead budget split per line, an approval lock and versioned revisions. Interim payment certificates certify cumulative work done per line, withhold retention (percentage, capped) and raise the net customer invoice.

Change events become priced variation orders that, once approved, append their lines to the BOQ — adjusting the contract value even when the bill is locked — and flow into the next certificate. Cost Value Reconciliation then puts contract value and certified value against budget, committed and incurred cost, giving earned margin, forecast final margin, and the movement against the margin the job was tendered at. Overspend already signed away in subcontracts shows up in the forecast, not in next year's accounts.

Control

Approvals enforced in the method, not on the button

A groups attribute on a view hides a control; it does not stop the method being called. Majal's approval engine checks inside action_approve itself, so the rule holds whether the call comes from a button, a script or RPC.

Rules match a document's kind, project and value band to a chain of signatures — a variation under 50,000 takes one, one above 250,000 takes three — configured as data, not code. Segregation of duties stops the raiser signing their own document. Steps are ordered. Rejections require a reason. Delegation records whose authority was used rather than who was logged in. And because each step is a record, "waiting for me" is one inbox across every wired document type.

Why the enforcement point matters →

Field

Pins on drawings, and a day that fits on a phone

Sheet PDFs render on a canvas in the browser with status-coloured pins; three taps turn a point on a drawing into a task, RFI, defect or note. Defects carry photos and route to the subcontractor who has to fix them. Inspections are built from no-code checklist templates with photo and signature evidence, and recur on a schedule. Daily logs record weather, manpower, plant, activities and delays.

My Day collects everything assigned to one person — approvals waiting on them, their defects, inspections, tasks, RFIs and permits — on one screen, ordered by how much trouble it causes to ignore. Rows are 64 px tall, and the screen was measured at a real 390 px viewport rather than assumed.

BIM

A model that is a record, not a picture

IFC files are indexed server-side by a dependency-free STEP reader — elements, property sets and quantities — with no third-party library and no geometry toolchain on the server. Links between records and elements are keyed on the GlobalId, the one identity IFC keeps stable across exports, so an RFI raised against a wall survives the model being re-issued. An element that disappears while carrying records is flagged, never silently deleted.

On top of the index: a browser viewer with storey filtering, section cuts and 3D pins; 4D driven by the dates of the programme tasks elements are linked to; BCF 2.1 round-trip with Solibri, Navisworks, BIMcollab and Revizto; revision comparison; federation by discipline; and a bounding-box clash test with a status workflow, so a clash somebody approved stays quiet on every future run.

Facilities

CAFM that knows what a promise costs

Assets sit in a site → building → floor → room hierarchy with criticality, warranties, meters, failure codes and downtime. Preventive maintenance plans — calendar- and meter-based — generate work orders by cron, each carrying a checklist copied from its job plan.

SLAs are matched from a policy matrix on priority, maintenance type, equipment category and asset criticality, with response and resolution clocks measured on a business calendar so a promise does not burn overnight. Annual maintenance contracts carry the SLA that was sold, keep absorbed and recoverable cost apart, and show a live margin against what they have invoiced.

Ownership

Your server, your database, your source

Odoo 18 Community (LGPL-3) pinned by commit, OCA modules pinned by revision, and the Majal suite itself — 35 modules declared LGPL-3, two declared AGPL-3. Portal accounts for subcontractors, clients and consultants are free Odoo portal users, so the people who only need to answer an RFI or accept a defect do not arrive as a per-seat line item.

Backup is seven curated recovery slots, each verified with SHA-256, restored through an audited two-step offline procedure that a live web process cannot trigger on its own. English and Arabic ship together, with RTL handled in the interface rather than left to the browser.

One screen for the person on site

The data was always there. Defects carry an assignee, inspections an inspector, tasks a user, RFIs a ball-in-court, permits a supervisor, approvals a step. But a register is the right shape for filing a document and the wrong shape for starting a day — so a site engineer opened five registers and filtered each by hand, and the fifth one did not get opened.

And one for the person who has to answer the board

A dashboard of counts answers "how busy are we". Commercial Exposure answers "what are we exposed to": contract value across live projects, approved variations and what percentage of the contract they represent, retention held, and work certified but not yet invoiced — then a separate banner for variations submitted and not yet approved, because exposure already taken and exposure about to arrive are different questions.

Built against a known bar

Four platforms set the standard Majal was designed against, and the design notes say plainly what each one does better: Procore as the enterprise system of record, Fieldwire as the field-first tool crews actually open, PlanRadar for pin-on-plan documentation, and IBM Maximo for enterprise asset management.

The gap Majal aims at is the one common to the field tools: data captured on site never reaches the ledger. Odoo Community supplies the ledger; the construction commercial layer — BOQ to subcontract to variation to progress claim with retention — is the piece missing from every field tool and from Odoo core; and free portal users are PlanRadar's adoption lever without the per-seat maths.
