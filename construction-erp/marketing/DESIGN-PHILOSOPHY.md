# Load Path

*A visual philosophy for the Majal collateral system.*

---

## The movement

**Load Path** takes its name from the engineer's question about any structure:
where does the weight actually go? Not where does it look like it goes — where
does it *go*. Every honest building has one, drawn or not, and the discipline of
the movement is to make that line visible and then trust it to carry the
composition. Nothing decorative bears load. Nothing that bears load is hidden.

A page built this way begins with a single continuous spine — a hairline that
descends, turns, and terminates — and everything else is hung from it,
cantilevered off it, or deliberately left in the void beside it. The spine is
never ornamental and never symmetrical for its own sake. It is a diagram of
consequence: this thing happened, therefore that thing followed. Where the line
changes direction, something is at stake, and the eye is told so by a single
gold node no larger than a pin head. Restraint at the node is the whole craft.
A designer at the top of their field earns the right to place one mark by
removing forty others, and the removals must be as painstaking as the placement.

## Space and form

Space is structural, not atmospheric. The page is divided by a strict modular
grid whose columns are visible only in the alignments they enforce — margins
are wide enough to read as deliberate quiet rather than as leftover, and the
outermost twelve percent of every edge is inviolable. Forms are orthogonal:
rectangles, hairlines, and the single obtuse gable that recurs as the system's
one non-rectilinear gesture, a roofline over an implied volume. Curves are
forbidden except where a radius is functional. Voids are sized, measured, and
defended; an empty band across two-thirds of a page is not absence but a bay
left open on purpose, and it must look like a decision that cost the designer
something.

## Colour and material

The palette is a deep marine ground, a working teal, a single warm metal, and
paper. The ground is where structure lives; the metal marks only load points,
rules, and reference. Metal never sets running text on light paper — it is a
mark, not a voice. Between ground and paper sits one working mid-tone that
carries diagrams, ticks, and the quiet supporting text. Colour is never
mood-setting and never gradient-driven; where a gradient appears it is a single
slow raking of the ground, as if light fell across concrete once, and it is so
subtle that a viewer notices only that the surface feels physical. The material
reference throughout is drawing paper, drafting film, and cold-rolled section:
matte, dense, faintly tooled.

## Scale and rhythm

Typography is a measured system with four voices and no more: a tall condensed
display face used at architectural scale and set tight, a neutral grotesque for
the reading register, a monospace used only for reference markers, units and
codes, and a bilingual companion whose Arabic is set with the same authority as
its Latin — mirrored, not merely translated, with the spine flipped and the
metal rule moved to the leading edge. Everything sits on one baseline unit and
every vertical measure is an integer multiple of it, so the rhythm of a poster
and the rhythm of page seven of a document are the same rhythm. Hierarchy is
achieved by scale ratio and weight, never by more than two type sizes competing
in the same field. Small text is *small* — six or seven points of monospace,
tracked open, sitting on a hairline — because confidence reads at the bottom of
the page, not the top.

## Composition and hierarchy

Each surface carries exactly one idea. The idea is stated once, in the display
voice, and everything else on the page exists to prove or locate it. Supporting
material is arrayed as systematic notation: repeated ticks, indexed rows,
enumerated modules, a run of identical marks whose accumulation does the
persuading. This is the visual language of the field notebook and the
specification sheet — patient repetition that rewards a second and third
viewing, and that suggests a discipline behind the page rather than a campaign.
Where evidence cannot be shown, the space for it is drawn and labelled rather
than filled with invention; an honest empty frame is part of the aesthetic, and
a fabricated one would violate it.

## The standard

Load Path is unforgiving about execution. Alignments must be exact to the unit,
not approximately right. No element may overlap another; nothing may crowd an
edge; every optical relationship must be checked against the mathematical one
and the optical must win. The finished set should read as though a single hand
laboured over it for weeks — a hand that knows what a bill of quantities is,
what a critical path costs, and what it means to sign something. Master-level
execution here is not flourish but the total absence of accident: every hairline
the same weight it was on the first page, every gold node the same diameter,
every margin the same to a tenth of a millimetre. The work must survive being
looked at closely by someone who builds things for a living.
